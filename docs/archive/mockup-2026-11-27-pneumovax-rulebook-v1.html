Reference

PneumoVax decisions E14

Where CDC and ACIP give no answer, PneumoVax chose one. Each is listed once and marked wherever it applies.

Transplant stop
Problem: Stem cell transplant, CAR-T therapy, B-cell malignancy or B-cell-depleting therapy. Revaccination is individualized.
CDC and ACIP say: A separate schedule applies, outside the routine and risk-based schedules.
PneumoVax decision: No recommendation is shown, even with another condition selected. Follow institutional guidelines (ASCO, NCCN, IDSA, CDC).
Applies in: Step 1, Stop check
A PCV given when none was due E6
Problem: A child received a PCV the card did not recommend: PCV15 where PCV20 or PPSV23 was offered, a PCV after the series was complete, or a PCV for a healthy child for whom it was not indicated.
CDC and ACIP say: PCV20 or PPSV23 at this point, and nothing after a completed series. They do not say how to count a PCV given anyway.
PneumoVax decision: Labelled "Not counted — Extra. Do not repeat." A completed series stays complete. A dose still owed is given ≥8 weeks after the extra PCV. Not applied to a healthy child's first dose, PCV13, an at-risk child under 6 years, or from 19 years.
Applies in: Step 2, A dose that does not count
PPSV23 not repeated
Problem: A PPSV23 did not count: the first was given <8 weeks after a PCV, or two PPSV23 doses both did not count. Each additional PPSV23 dose produces a weaker immune response to some serotypes.
CDC and ACIP say: PPSV23 at this point, as for a patient who has had none. They do not say what to give when a PPSV23 on record did not count.
PneumoVax decision: PCV20 instead (adults: PCV20 or PCV21). Not PPSV23, and not PCV15, which would require PPSV23 after it.
Applies in: Step 2, A dose that does not count · Step 4, Prior pneumococcal vaccines
Invalid PPSV23 still sets the timing
Problem: A PPSV23 did not count (given before 2 years, or <8 weeks after a PCV), but the patient received it.
CDC and ACIP say: Intervals are measured from a PPSV23. They do not say whether one that did not count starts the interval.
PneumoVax decision: The next dose is timed from it. Children: PCV ≥8 weeks after it (a PCV given sooner still counts); a repeat PPSV23 ≥5 years after it. Adults: PCV ≥1 year after it.
Applies in: Step 2, A dose that does not count · Step 4, Prior pneumococcal vaccines · Step 5, children's and adults' tables
Next dose falls after 19 years
Problem: A child's next dose falls at 19 years or later, when the adult schedule may differ: no dose, another product, or a longer interval.
CDC and ACIP say: The child schedule until 19 years, the adult schedule after. They do not say which governs a dose the child schedule places after 19 years.
PneumoVax decision: The card gives the adult schedule's answer and states why. A dose that can be given before 19 years stays on the child schedule.
Applies in: Step 3, From 19 years
PCV too soon after PPSV23
Problem: A child's PCV was given <8 weeks after a PPSV23 that counted. A PCV given soon after PPSV23 produces a weaker immune response.
CDC and ACIP say: PCV ≥8 weeks after PPSV23. They do not say whether a PCV given sooner counts.
PneumoVax decision: Not counted. If the series is complete without it, nothing more is given. If a PCV is still needed, it is given ≥8 weeks after the one that did not count.
Applies in: Step 5, children's PCV and PPSV23 table
Early 2nd PPSV23 still counts
Problem: An immunocompromised child's 2nd PPSV23 was given <5 years after the 1st. Repeating it would mean a 3rd PPSV23.
CDC and ACIP say: The 2nd PPSV23 ≥5 years after the 1st. They do not say whether one given sooner counts.
PneumoVax decision: Counted and not repeated. The line under the dose states the interval given and the 5 years recommended. No further PPSV23.
Applies in: Step 5, children's PCV and PPSV23 table
An early adult dose still counts E15
Problem: An adult dose was given sooner than recommended after an earlier dose, for example PCV20 3 months after PPSV23, where 1 year is recommended.
CDC and ACIP say: Adult intervals are recommended timing: 8 weeks for PPSV23 after a PCV with an immunocompromising condition, cochlear implant or CSF leak (19–49 years; an option from 50 years); 1 year for PPSV23 after a PCV in other adults, PCV20 or PCV21 after PCV13, and any PCV after PPSV23; 5 years for PCV20 or PCV21 after PCV13 and PPSV23, and for a second PPSV23. They do not say an earlier dose is invalid or must be repeated. CDC's immunization software sets no minimum for any adult interval.
PneumoVax decision: The dose counts and is not repeated. The line under the dose states the interval, for example "Given 3 months after the PPSV23 dose of Jan 10, 2026. CDC recommends 1 year." Exceptions with their own entries: PPSV23 under 8 weeks after a PCV; Adult PCV after PCV. A second PPSV23 without an immunocompromising condition, cochlear implant or CSF leak is labelled "Counted — not needed. No more PPSV23."
Applies in: Step 5, adults' table
PPSV23 under 8 weeks after a PCV E15
Problem: An adult's PPSV23 was given <8 weeks after a PCV. ACIP calls the 8 weeks a "minimum interval", unlike any other adult interval.
CDC and ACIP say: "A minimum interval of 8 weeks can be considered" for an immunocompromising condition, cochlear implant or CSF leak. This allows PPSV23 sooner than the usual 1 year. They do not say whether a PPSV23 given before 8 weeks counts.
PneumoVax decision: Counted and not repeated, because another PPSV23 produces a weaker immune response to some serotypes. The line under the dose states the interval, for example "Given on the same day as the PCV15 dose of Jan 10, 2026. ACIP recommends 8 weeks (56 days) for this patient."
Applies in: Step 5, adults' table, row "PCV, then PPSV23"
Adult PCV after PCV
Problem: An adult received a PCV <4 weeks after another PCV, for example PCV20 after an unrecorded PCV15. Other ACIP schedules (infants, transplant recipients) space PCV doses ≥4 weeks apart; there are no data on a shorter interval.
CDC and ACIP say: No interval between two adult PCV doses.
PneumoVax decision: Counted at any interval. Under 4 weeks, the line under the dose offers an optional repeat (PCV20 or PCV21) ≥8 weeks later, mainly for an immunocompromised patient.
Applies in: Step 5, adults' table
No dose now, not Complete
Problem: Every dose due today has been given, and a further dose is due later on the current schedule. "Complete" would imply nothing more is needed.
CDC and ACIP say: Give the later dose. They do not say how to describe the visits before it.
PneumoVax decision: Status "No dose now", with the later dose shown. "Complete" means nothing further on the current schedule. A child who finishes the child schedule stays Complete, even if the adult schedule adds a dose at 19 years.
Applies in: Step 4, Prior pneumococcal vaccines
